A public university built for Adversary City.

City University opened in 1891 to train the teachers, engineers, and clerks a growing Adversary City needed. It's now a six-college public research university with 24,600 students, still governed by the same charter and still funded in part by the city it's named for.

From a teachers' college to a research university.

City University opened its doors in 1891 as the Adversary City Normal School, a two-year program for training public school teachers. The state charter that founded it still governs the university's board of trustees today.

The school added engineering and commerce departments in 1924 and was rechartered as City University in 1931. It began granting doctoral degrees in 1962, when the Graduate School was established, and opened the College of Health Sciences in 1978 to train nurses for the city's growing hospital system.

The most recent addition, the Engineering research annex, opened in 2019 and houses the robotics and automation lab referenced in the course catalog. A seventh college, focused on public health, is under review by the board of trustees for 2029.

What the university is chartered to do.

City University's 1891 charter commits it to affordable public education for Adversary City residents, first, and the wider region, second. Tuition for in-city residents is set below the regional average by board policy.
